The launchd board for your Macs.

See your Mac’s launchd services in one window: what’s running, what’s waiting or failed, and which TCP ports they listen on. Add your other Macs over SSH to view them too.

What it shows, and what it lets you do

Running isn’t the same as healthy.

Launch Board keeps two answers apart: is launchd running the service, and is the service actually working. Health checks aren’t built yet, so health stays Unverified. A running service never gets a green light by default.

Your other Macs, read-only

Add other Macs over SSH and see their services next to this one’s. Remote Macs are for looking, never changing.

Start, restart, stop

Control your own LaunchAgents from the window. Every action asks you to confirm first.

CPU, memory and ports

CPU and RSS history for each service, and the TCP ports it’s listening on.

Groups, favorites and search

Group services by project, star the ones you check every day, and find any label as you type.

In the menu bar

See what needs attention from the menu bar without opening the window.

Nothing sent anywhere

No analytics, no telemetry, no account. The only connections it makes are the SSH sessions you set up.

A free, open-source alternative to paid launchd GUIs — with multi-Mac viewing.

Where it stops

Launch Board watches more than it touches. These limits are on purpose.

  • System services are read-onlyOnly your own LaunchAgents can be controlled. System services are never started, stopped or edited, and for LaunchDaemons it reads the definition only and shows their state as Unverified.
  • No remote controlOther Macs are for viewing. To change a service there, do it on that Mac.
  • No plist deletionLaunch Board never deletes a plist file. Removing a service stays your call.

Questions

Why isn’t it on the App Store?

App Store apps run in a sandbox, and the sandbox doesn’t allow controlling services through launchctl. So Launch Board is distributed directly, from GitHub and Homebrew.

What permissions does it need?

Reading your own services needs nothing special. Adding another Mac uses the SSH access you already have to it. Launch Board asks before it starts, restarts or stops anything.

How do I uninstall it?

If you registered the collector, turn it off in Settings first. Then quit Launch Board and move it to the Trash. With Homebrew, run brew uninstall launch-board.

Does it collect any data?

No. There’s no analytics, crash reporting or account. CPU and memory history stays on your Mac.
